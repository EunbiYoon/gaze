Visual search learned without box labels

We train a PPO gaze policy (saccade / zoom / stop) rewarded by CLIP text similarity, and add human gaze (COCO-Search18 fixations) through a CGL-style coverage loss, to see whether target-finding improves without boxes. Boxes are used only for evaluation.

Where we are

Gate 1 · Passed

The CLIP signal is usable. At equal glimpse counts, CLIP-greedy beats random by 25–35pp (1 glimpse: 33.4% vs 4.2%).

Gate 2 · Not passed (preliminary)

RL does not beat CLIP-greedy. At the same budget of 24, RL clip_full reaches 50.4% vs greedy 67.9%.

CGL effect · Negative (preliminary)

The human-gaze loss hurts. clip_full_cgl − clip_full = −8.8pp, 95% CI [−14.9, −2.7], p=0.016.

Preliminary: 2 of 5 folds (folds 0 and 1), a single seed (0), 286 held-out images. These are not final numbers.

Interpretation

  • Undertrained, not overfit. Train success (≈53%) and held-out success (≈50%) are close. The learning curve is still rising at iter 400, and one run takes only about 77 seconds.
  • Steps and glimpses are not the same unit. RL starts on a 4×4 grid and must zoom to reach the finest cells, so success is 0 at 1–2 steps. A greedy glimpse sees a fine cell directly. Keep this in mind when comparing.
  • At large budgets the ceiling is “choosing”, not “finding”. At 64 glimpses greedy has seen the target cell 94% of the time but answers correctly only 69%. The remaining headroom is in the small-budget range (1–16).

Principles

  • Hyperparameters and prompts are never changed after looking at test results. Only κ is selected, on the train fold. Training-setting diagnostics such as iters are judged only by train success.
  • The fair comparison is “accuracy at equal glimpse count”. CLIP full argmax and a box-trained detector (Faster R-CNN) are upper-bound references.
  • Data: COCO-Search18 split1 train+valid (713 target-present images, 18 categories). split2 is a different split of the same data and is not mixed in. Waldo data is not used.
